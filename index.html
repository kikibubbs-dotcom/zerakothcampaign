✦ CORE MECHANICS QUICK REFERENCE

Practical rules notes for your mixed 2014 / 2024 campaign. Check the character's selected edition where rules differ.

Languages: standard & sign languages

Common: the trade language widely shared across many regions. Dwarvish: traditional language of dwarven communities. Elvish: associated with elven cultures. Giant: used by giants and related peoples. Gnomish: traditional gnomish language. Goblin: used by goblinoid peoples. Halfling: traditional halfling language. Orc: used by orc communities. The 2024 rules also include Common Sign Language as a standard language option.

Record each character's known languages on their sheet. A language may have spoken, signed and/or written forms; do not assume every creature can read or write a language it speaks.

Languages: exotic languages

Abyssal: demons and the Abyss. Celestial: celestials. Draconic: dragons and dragon-related traditions. Deep Speech: aberrations and alien entities. Infernal: devils and the Nine Hells. Primordial: elemental beings, with Aquan, Auran, Ignan and Terran dialects. Sylvan: fey. Undercommon: the language of many Underdark communities.

Language lists can vary by source and setting. Your campaign's chosen sourcebook or homebrew lore takes precedence.

Conditions: blinded, charmed & deafened

Blinded: cannot see; automatically fails ability checks requiring sight. Attacks have disadvantage, and attacks against the creature have advantage. Charmed: cannot attack the charmer or target the charmer with harmful abilities or magical effects; the charmer has advantage on relevant social checks. Deafened: cannot hear and automatically fails ability checks requiring hearing.

Conditions: frightened, grappled & incapacitated

Frightened: disadvantage on ability checks and attack rolls while the source of fear is in line of sight; cannot willingly move closer to it. Grappled: Speed becomes 0 and cannot benefit from bonuses to Speed; ends if the grappler is incapacitated or the effect moves the creature away. Incapacitated: cannot take actions or reactions.

Conditions: invisible, paralyzed & petrified

Invisible: impossible to see without special sense or magic; treated as heavily obscured for hiding. Attacks have advantage, and attacks against the creature have disadvantage. Paralyzed: incapacitated, cannot move or speak, automatically fails Strength and Dexterity saves; attacks against it have advantage, and hits from within 5 feet are critical hits. Petrified: transformed into a solid inanimate substance; incapacitated, unaware, cannot move or speak, has resistance to all damage and immunity to poison and disease, and automatically fails Strength and Dexterity saves.

Conditions: poisoned, prone & restrained

Poisoned: disadvantage on attack rolls and ability checks. Prone: can crawl or stand by spending movement; attacks have disadvantage, attacks against it have advantage within 5 feet and disadvantage from farther away. Restrained: Speed becomes 0; attacks have disadvantage, attacks against it have advantage, and it has disadvantage on Dexterity saves.

Conditions: stunned & unconscious

Stunned: incapacitated, cannot move and can speak only falteringly; automatically fails Strength and Dexterity saves; attacks against it have advantage. Unconscious: incapacitated, cannot move or speak, unaware, drops what it is holding and falls prone; automatically fails Strength and Dexterity saves; attacks against it have advantage, and hits from within 5 feet are critical hits.

Turn structure & actions

On your turn, you normally move up to your Speed and take one action. You may also have one bonus action if a feature or spell grants one. You can take one reaction between the starts of your turns. Common actions include Attack, Dash, Disengage, Dodge, Help, Hide, Ready, Search, Study (2024), Utilize (2024), and Magic (2024).

2014: Object interaction is generally one free interaction on your turn; additional interactions may require the Use an Object action. 2024: the Utilize action handles many nonmagical object interactions.

Opportunity attacks & movement

Moving out of an enemy's reach can provoke an opportunity attack when that enemy can see you. Disengage prevents opportunity attacks for the rest of your turn. Difficult terrain costs extra movement. Standing from prone costs movement equal to half your Speed. Crawling usually costs extra movement.

Cover & hiding

Half cover: +2 AC and Dexterity saves. Three-quarters cover: +5 AC and Dexterity saves. Total cover: cannot be targeted directly by an attack or spell from the other side of the cover. Hiding requires an appropriate situation and a successful Dexterity (Stealth) check against the relevant observer's passive Perception or active check.

Ability checks, saves & proficiency

Roll a d20 and add the relevant ability modifier. Add proficiency bonus when proficient. Advantage means roll two d20s and use the higher; disadvantage means use the lower. Multiple sources of advantage or disadvantage do not stack; if both apply, they cancel.

Concentration & spellcasting

You can concentrate on only one spell at a time. Taking damage normally requires a Constitution saving throw to maintain concentration (DC 10 or half the damage taken, whichever is higher). Casting another concentration spell ends the previous one. Verbal, Somatic and Material components must be supplied as the spell requires; a component pouch or appropriate focus can replace eligible non-costly, non-consumed material components.

Death saves & dying

At 0 HP, a character normally falls unconscious and begins making death saving throws at the start of their turns. A 10 or higher is a success; 9 or lower is a failure. Three successes stabilise the character; three failures cause death. A natural 20 restores 1 HP; a natural 1 counts as two failures. Damage at 0 HP can cause failures, with a critical hit counting as two.

Exhaustion: 2014 vs 2024

2014: six levels with cumulative penalties: disadvantage on ability checks, then Speed halved, then disadvantage on attacks and saves, then HP maximum halved, then Speed reduced to 0, then death. A long rest with food and drink reduces exhaustion by one level.

2024: ten levels. Each level imposes a −2 penalty to D20 Tests; Speed is reduced by 5 feet per level. At level 10, the creature dies. A long rest reduces exhaustion by one level.

Short rests & long rests

2014: a short rest is at least 1 hour; spend Hit Dice to regain HP. A long rest is at least 8 hours and restores all HP, spell slots and up to half your total Hit Dice (minimum one). 2024: a short rest is at least 1 hour; a long rest is at least 8 hours and restores all HP, all spent Hit Dice and one exhaustion level. Your campaign hub applies the edition selected on each character.

Conditions overview

Blinded, Charmed, Deafened, Exhaustion, Frightened, Grappled, Incapacitated, Invisible, Paralyzed, Petrified, Poisoned, Prone, Restrained, Stunned and Unconscious have specific effects. Use the Conditions category above for individual descriptions and the correct edition's wording.

Languages & communication

Languages determine spoken, signed or written communication where specified. A creature may understand a language without being able to speak it. Some features grant additional languages; telepathy and magical communication may bypass ordinary language barriers. Use the Languages category above to browse SRD language entries and record known languages on each character.

Ability scores & modifiers

Ability modifiers are added to relevant checks, saves and attacks: 8–9 = −1, 10–11 = +0, 12–13 = +1, 14–15 = +2, 16–17 = +3 and 18–19 = +4. A natural 20 or 1 is not automatically a success or failure on an ability check or saving throw unless a specific rule says so.

Skills & passive checks

Skills use an associated ability: Acrobatics (Dex), Animal Handling (Wis), Arcana (Int), Athletics (Str), Deception (Cha), History (Int), Insight (Wis), Intimidation (Cha), Investigation (Int), Medicine (Wis), Nature (Int), Perception (Wis), Performance (Cha), Persuasion (Cha), Religion (Int), Sleight of Hand (Dex), Stealth (Dex) and Survival (Wis). A passive check is usually 10 + applicable modifiers; advantage adds 5 and disadvantage subtracts 5.

Initiative, surprise & rounds

Initiative determines turn order, normally using Dexterity. A round represents about 6 seconds. Surprise is determined when combat begins if a creature is unaware of a threat; apply the selected edition's procedure rather than assuming a separate surprise round.

Attacks, AC & damage

An attack roll is a d20 + the relevant ability modifier + proficiency bonus when proficient. It hits if it equals or exceeds AC. A natural 20 on an attack is a critical hit; a natural 1 misses. Resistance halves relevant damage, vulnerability doubles it and immunity prevents it.

Spell attacks & saving throws

Spell save DC is usually 8 + proficiency bonus + spellcasting ability modifier. Spell attack bonus is proficiency bonus + spellcasting ability modifier. The target makes the saving throw named by the spell; compare the result with the caster's spell save DC. The spell text controls range, targets, components, duration and effects.

Grappling & shoving

In 2014, grappling and shoving are special melee attacks resolved with contested checks. In 2024, they are options within an Unarmed Strike and use a saving throw. A grapple normally sets the target's Speed to 0; escaping and shove effects follow the selected edition's rules.

Falling & environmental hazards

Falling, extreme temperatures, suffocation, drowning, starvation, thirst and other hazards use specific rules or DM adjudication. Check the chosen rules edition and campaign house rules, and make ongoing hazards clear so players understand how to avoid or end them.

Conditions: common effects

Blinded: can't see; attacks against you have advantage and your attacks have disadvantage. Charmed: can't attack or harm the charmer; the charmer has advantage on social checks. Deafened: can't hear. Frightened: disadvantage on checks and attacks while the source is in sight; can't willingly move closer. Grappled: Speed 0. Incapacitated: can't take actions or reactions. Invisible: unseen; attacks against you have disadvantage and your attacks have advantage. Prone: crawling costs extra movement; standing costs half Speed; nearby melee attacks have advantage and ranged attacks disadvantage. Restrained: Speed 0; attacks against you have advantage, your attacks and Dexterity saves have disadvantage. Stunned: incapacitated, can't move and automatically fails Strength and Dexterity saves. Unconscious: incapacitated, prone and unaware; drops held items.

Conditions: other effects

Paralyzed: incapacitated, can't move or speak, automatically fails Strength and Dexterity saves; attacks from within 5 feet that hit are critical hits. Petrified: transformed into a solid inanimate substance and incapacitated, with additional effects specified by the condition. Poisoned: disadvantage on attack rolls and ability checks. Exhaustion: penalties differ between editions (see above). Conditions can overlap; each ends only when its stated duration or ending requirement is met.

Languages: standard & exotic

Common standard languages include Common, Dwarvish, Elvish, Giant, Gnomish, Goblin, Halfling and Orc. Exotic languages can include Abyssal, Celestial, Draconic, Deep Speech, Infernal, Primordial, Sylvan and Undercommon. The available list depends on the setting and source. A language's script, spoken form and any dialects are distinct from telepathy or magical communication.
